The portfolio owner is a **senior full-stack software engineer** whose paid experience is backend-heavy. The portfolio's audience is **recruiters, hiring managers, and — critically — senior designers / creative directors**.

**The portfolio's PURPOSE (corrected 2026-06-06): prove the owner is an exceptional FRONT-END and DESIGN talent.** It must NOT highlight backend skills — the owner considers their resume/experience to already cover that. The portfolio is the counter-argument to "they're just a backend dev who learned some CSS." This explicitly reverses the earlier assumption that backend identity should be legible in the work.

Key traits and constraints:

- Wants the portfolio to demonstrate **extraordinary frontend + visual-design craft** — animation systems, typography, creative layout, motion/interaction design, shaders, generative art, etc. The wow must read as _taste and design sensibility_, not just technical tricks.
- The bar: a senior designer or creative director should stop scrolling and ask "who built this?" — not "neat CSS trick."
- **Accessibility is a hard, non-negotiable requirement** (WCAG 2.2 AA baseline). Concepts that can't be made accessible are disqualified, not caveated.
- Has shipped ambitious work before — the previous portfolio **recreated a minimal Ubuntu desktop environment in the browser**. New work must clear that bar but be entirely different.
- Comfortable with ambitious technical builds; do not water down ideas to be "safe." Be opinionated with this user.

How to apply: Favor concepts where the wow factor IS the design artifact. Do NOT frame the portfolio around backend/systems metaphors. See [[portfolio-concept-direction]].
